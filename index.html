🔢 Numbers & Number Types

Mathematics begins with numbers. In nature and in technology we constantly encounter different kinds of numbers.

Key Number Types (College Review)

  • Natural numbers (ℕ): 1, 2, 3, … — counting objects
  • Whole numbers: 0, 1, 2, 3, …
  • Integers (ℤ): …, −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, …
  • Rational numbers (ℚ): fractions and terminating/repeating decimals (e.g., 3/4, 0.333…)
  • Irrational numbers: non-repeating, non-terminating (√2, π, φ ≈ 1.618…)
  • Real numbers (ℝ): all rational + irrational

🔍 Patterns in Nature

A pattern is a regular, repeated arrangement of numbers, shapes, colors, or other elements that follows a rule. Nature is rich with patterns that can be modeled mathematically.

Common Natural Patterns

  • Symmetry — bilateral (butterfly) or radial (flower)
  • Spirals — sunflower seed heads, pinecones, nautilus shells
  • Branching — trees, rivers, blood vessels
  • Numerical sequences — leaf arrangements (phyllotaxis), population growth models

Important teaching note: Natural objects are models of mathematical patterns. Not every sunflower follows a perfect Fibonacci arrangement, but the ideal model helps us understand the underlying mathematics.

📐 Introduction to Sequences

A sequence is an ordered list of numbers that follows a definite rule. Each number in the sequence is called a term.

We write a sequence as: a₁, a₂, a₃, …, aₙ

Four Important Sequences in this Module

  • Arithmetic Sequence — constant difference between consecutive terms
  • Geometric Sequence — constant ratio between consecutive terms
  • Harmonic Sequence — reciprocals of an arithmetic sequence
  • Fibonacci Sequence — each term is the sum of the two preceding terms

➕ Arithmetic Sequence

An arithmetic sequence has a constant difference d between consecutive terms. This difference is called the common difference.

General term: aₙ = a₁ + (n − 1)d

Example from Nature

A simplified model of a plant’s branch lengths (in cm) follows:

3, 7, 11, 15, 19, …

Common difference d = 4. The next term is 19 + 4 = 23.

✖️ Geometric Sequence

A geometric sequence has a constant ratio r between consecutive terms. This is the common ratio.

General term: aₙ = a₁ · r(n−1)

Example

Bacterial colony size (simplified) doubles every generation:

4, 8, 16, 32, 64, …

Common ratio r = 2.

➗ Harmonic Sequence

A harmonic sequence is formed by taking the reciprocals of the terms of an arithmetic sequence.

If a, a+d, a+2d, … is arithmetic, then

1/a , 1/(a+d) , 1/(a+2d) , …

is harmonic.

Classic Example

1, 1/2, 1/3, 1/4, 1/5, …

This is the harmonic sequence corresponding to the arithmetic sequence 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, …

🌻 Fibonacci Sequence & Fibonacci Garden

The Fibonacci sequence is defined recursively:

F₁ = 1, F₂ = 1, Fₙ = Fₙ₋₁ + Fₙ₋₂ for n ≥ 3

So the sequence begins: 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21, 34, …

This sequence famously appears in idealized models of phyllotaxis (leaf and seed arrangement), spiral shells, and branching patterns.

⚙️ Engineering & Technology Applications

Sequences are not just abstract; they appear throughout engineering and technology:

  • Arithmetic — linear depreciation of equipment, uniform sampling intervals, stepped voltage levels
  • Geometric — compound interest / growth models, signal attenuation, recursive filters, population dynamics in systems
  • Harmonic — analysis of resistors in parallel, certain wave phenomena, musical intervals
  • Fibonacci / Golden Ratio — efficient packing algorithms, antenna design, architectural proportions, computer science (Fibonacci heaps, search algorithms)
